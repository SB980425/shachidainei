- Date: 2026-06-11
- Scope: current public AgentSiteOps site and Launch Blueprint offer
- Method: website-opportunity-scoring model, scored from 1 to 5 per field and normalized to 100
- Decision threshold: 70+ proceed, 55-69 pilot, below 55 stop or pivot

## Conclusion

Current score: **57 / 100**

Decision: **pilot only**

This site should not be treated as a validated business yet. The technical base, compliance boundary, and page system are usable. The weak points are search demand evidence, proof of authority, competitive moat, and paid conversion evidence.

## Direct Answer To The Pricing Problem

The USD 99 price is not objectively proven. It can only be treated as a validation price.

Compared with AI-search or SEO platforms, AgentSiteOps does not compete on crawlers, dashboards, prompt databases, rank tracking, or long-term monitoring. Those products are better for ongoing measurement.

AgentSiteOps only has a valid reason to charge if the buyer needs a manually compressed route: one offer, one buyer, one page structure, one price boundary, and one first outreach sequence. If buyers do not respond to that promise, the product should be repackaged or lowered.

## Current Weak Points

1. No real search platform data yet.
2. No customer proof or public case study from an external buyer.
3. No proprietary demand dataset.
4. No evidence that USD 99 converts.
5. No direct comparison page proving when AgentSiteOps is better than a generic AI chat or SEO software.
6. No automated delivery or order tracking.
7. The product is still closer to a manual consulting artifact than software.

## Next Objective Plan

1. Keep only the live USD 99 payment route and treat it as a validation hypothesis, not a proven price.
2. Track qualified purchase intent before adding a lower or higher paid tier.
3. Add a comparison page explaining when to use AgentSiteOps, generic AI chat, SEO software, or AI-search monitoring software.
4. Add a stronger sample blueprint with bad input, scoring, rejected paths, final route, and stop rules.
5. Set a 7-day decision gate:
   - keep USD 99 if there is one paid purchase or three qualified purchase-intent contacts;
   - test a lower front-end offer if there are clicks but no purchase;
   - pivot the offer if there are no clicks or qualified messages.

## Stop / Pivot Rule

If there is no qualified purchase intent after the first validation window, the project should not keep defending the USD 99 offer. The next test should be a lower-friction product, such as a small paid diagnostic or a free self-score tool with a paid manual upgrade.
